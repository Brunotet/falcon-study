# Falcon strategy: rulebook, part 6 (remaining lessons, key-passage extraction)

Source note: S1E5, S2E6, S2E7, S2 Long Term Holds, foundation ep 3 pt 3, ep 2, ep 7, ep 9, ep 6, ep 1 and the trailer were processed by extracting the rule-bearing sentences, not read line by line. Ep 1, 6, 7, 9 and the trailer are mostly basics, journaling, broker and psychology. **[CODE]** = scanner-ready. **[OPEN]** = vague.

## 27. Phase identification (S1E5)
- Impulse and correction nest: corrections contain impulses and impulses contain corrections. A big impulse followed by a pattern is the pole and flag of a bigger continuation.
- You usually only know you are in a corrective phase **about halfway in**: a correction that retraces almost to the low where you first looked to buy was corrective. In an impulsive phase corrections are shallower and impulse again.
- After a corrective move up the next move is typically impulsive (and vice versa).
- In an impulsive phase the market may not pull back as deep as you expect. Be ready to enter early on the 4H/1H instead of waiting.
- Use the **most recent two touches** for the trendline; the third touch is not required.

## 28. Forecast drawing routine (S2E6)
- Draw the full plan on the chart before it happens: touches 1-2-3, the double bottom/top override, the flag (6-8 candles), stop (25-30 pips in the example), first target at the inflection point of the trendline. Pre-fill the deal ticket mentally so there is no hesitation. Look left at how the pattern played out before.
- Losses are not what hurts consistency; hesitating on the next valid setup is.

## 29. Mass psychology (S2E7, ep 3 pt 3)
- Edge = being on the opposite side of the crowd. Price breaks a previous high/low (the "broken support/resistance" retail sees), traps people, then returns into the pattern. This repeats across structures; look left for the same repeat.
- A break that is **corrective** (wedge, channel, no big candles) is more likely a trap and reverses. An **impulsive** break activates the 90% rule.
- Break of a high followed by return into the pattern = the "arc" / failed break. Do not chase the break with a market order; wait for the first impulse and first correction.
- **[CODE]** features: (a) prior swing high/low broken by less than X pips, (b) the break leg is corrective (small candles), (c) price closes back inside within N candles.

## 30. 90% rule, precisely (ep 3 pt 3, S2 long term holds)
- **Activation:** a structure (ascending or descending channel, daily or weekly) breaks **impulsively**, meaning a clear solid momentum close; a slightly smaller first candle is fine if the break is impulsive overall. Works both directions.
- Effect: about 90% probability that price fills about 90% of the correction, i.e. reaches roughly the **start of the correction** (usually fills 100%). The minimum target; use it to hold instead of banking at a nearby support.
- Question to ask: where does the correction begin? If it does not reach, "take a message from the market": use pattern separation, a smaller version may be forming.

## 31. Long-term holds (S2 long term holds)
- Still enter on the 1H (and 15m); the longer hold is a management style. Forecasts take weeks/months.
- At the top/bottom of a structure do not move to break even until the structure breaks cleanly (price can hover). Then lock profit structure-by-structure, not by money.
- Accept pullbacks of up to about 10-15% on trades whose full target is 30-60%. A trade can pull back, then continue. Leave one runner while scalping the inner 1H/15m moves.
- Pattern that suits holds: clean impulse, correction, big continuation flag with the 90% rule active.

## 32. Candles as override (ep 2)
- Candles are secondary to structure. Shapes used: **hanging man at the top** (sell signal in the right place, even if it looks bullish), **evening star / morning star**, **1H retrace candle** (usable for entry and for locking profit: when price retraces after a run, lock just below/above).
- Do not skip a good structure because a candle looks wrong.

## 33. Entry orders (ep 6)
- Use entry (stop) orders placed a few pips beyond the trigger, not market orders. A market order from fear of missing out gets spiked out for 1% and then price drops anyway.
- Instruments: 8 major currencies (AUD, CAD, CHF, EUR, GBP, JPY, NZD, USD) crossed into pairs. He never recommends a broker.

## 34. Journaling and testing (ep 7)
- Advanced self-review (ASR) per trade: entry, management, exit, higher-timeframe support, screenshots before and after. Tally monthly: trades, wins, losses, break evens, missed positions. Look for patterns such as repeated losses at tops/bottoms.
- Backtest per pair in a session (higher timeframe first, then lower, forecast ahead) and do not fantasize about 50-100% months. 6-12 months of consistent trading before taking outside capital.
- Count missed trades: one lesson was gaining confidence taking high-probability setups at tops/bottoms that need discretion (15m entry).

## 35. Psychology (ep 9, brief)
- Fear of missing out, tight irrelevant stops chosen to hit a profit number, revenge sizing and outcome focus are named failure modes. Process over outcome: check the trade against the plan before entry.

## 1. Core idea
Trendline and pattern trading on price structure. Structure first; candle shapes are a secondary "override". The edge is mass psychology: price breaks a previous high, traps people on the wrong side, then drops back into the pattern (mirror for lows). Think in probabilities, not right/wrong. Never rank trades A/B/C; use only **high probability** vs **valid**.

## 2. Timeframes
- Daily / 4H: the pattern, the phase, and the outer structure. 1H: entries and management (find setups on the 1H).
- 15m: only a filter (pattern separation), never the place to find setups.
- Before every trade, **zoom out** and check the higher timeframe.

## 3. Structure
- A valid trendline needs multi-touch identification (touches 1, 2, 3) and a **second touch where price moved off it**. A flag also needs its second point. The parallel (cloned) trendline of a channel is a useful reference.
- Look left: mark previous highs/lows. Best sells are at the top of a pattern, ideally **after the previous high is broken**.
- Patterns that are preferred: ascending/descending channels, double top/bottom within a pattern, impulse-correction-continuation, an "M"-type pattern into a double top.
- Weak: a big descending/sideways structure that can "trickle" for ages, or a continuation with a big scoop/gap. These play out 1-2 in 10 by his account.

### 3b. Pattern separation (S2E4)
- Draw the trendline through the **two most recent significant touches** of the current pattern.
- When price breaks a line, do not keep dragging the line outward to "evolve" it. Ask **how** it broke: a break in the form of a corrective/descending channel with no progression means the old line is still the structure; rein the line back to the latest two touches and see if a **new pattern** (new structure) forms. Keep old structure and new structure separate.
- A break of a trendline alone means nothing; how it breaks and what comes next does. Do not "marry" a trendline.
- A third touch on the separated line is the setup point.

### 3c. News (S2E5, foundation ep. 5)
- Structure over news. News creates volatility, not direction. Do not be paralysed by scheduled news.
- Beginners: avoid trading through major news. Elections: avoid. Slippage can exceed the 1% risk; accept it as a rare cost.
- **90% rule:** after an impulsive break of a structure, there is about a 90% chance price reaches the **start of the correction** (the origin of the move), unless it makes a deeper correction first.

## 4. Phase
- **Impulsive phase** (high already broken, first impulse back confirmed): setups here are higher probability.
- **Corrective phase** (inside the pattern, high not broken): same-looking setups are lower probability and may evolve higher first.
- Do not be put off by one impulse candle if ~80% of the move is corrective.
- **First correction after the first impulse = highest-probability flag.**

## 5. Valid vs high probability (S3E4, S3E5)
- **Valid structure is not a valid trade.** A valid pattern in no valid area (no outer structure, no double top, no higher-timeframe support) is skipped.
- **Valid trade:** valid structure in a valid area, but not fully supported by the higher timeframe. Still good, used as a hedge.
- **High probability:** at the outer structure / top or bottom of the pattern, or the completion/edge of a pattern, **in line with the higher timeframe**. Includes a one-two-three completion and ascending nature into a double top.
- Target ratio: **about 80% high probability, 20% valid**. Moving from 50/50 toward 80/20 is the goal.
- If not supported by the higher timeframe: **wait for the first impulse, then take the next correction.**
- Avoid fear-of-missing-out entries: do not place entries below a level just to get in early.
- Discretion should be only about 5-10% of the process.

## 6. Entry types
- **Risk entry:** anticipation at the top of the pattern, once price shows a rejection or low-momentum corrective candles. Nothing is confirmed yet.
- **Reduced-risk entry:** after confirmation, e.g. a Falcon flag (impulse, correction, impulse, retrace) in the impulsive phase.
- In a high-probability area do **not** wait for a perfect candle shape (e.g. evening star). Corrective price in the area is enough; place the entry and accept being tagged in.
- Skip a setup you cannot monitor.

## 7. 1H retrace candle
- Impulse candle then a candle that retraces it: valid if it retraces **more than 50%**; need not be the very next candle.
- Entry on the **body** (preferred) or below the wick. Works for entries and management.

## 8. Arc vs double top
- Arc = price slightly **above** the double top / third touch with corrective, low-momentum candles ("80/20").
- Do not discard the setup when price slightly exceeds the double top; it often evolves into a deeper ascending channel.
- Entry on the clear trendline break after the arc, ideally with a retrace candle.

## 8b. Order placement (foundation ep. 8)
- Use an **entry (stop) order** placed just beyond the trigger level (e.g. a few pips below the trendline/double-top break for a sell). Let price trigger you in. Market orders are generally avoided (too emotional); limit orders are not used.
- **No expiry** on the order; cancel it yourself when the setup is no longer valid.
- Order of work: forecast first, checklist (risk sized, target, higher-timeframe alignment), then place the order. Placing is the small part; analysis and planning are the important part.
- Take profit is usually **not** set; exits are managed with the stop (optional TP only if you cannot watch the screen).
- Position size: 1% of balance divided by (stop pips x pip value), via a position-size calculator.
- Manage from the chart/structure, not from the money going up and down.
- If tagged out at the top of a double top (e.g. managing actively with a tighter stop), **re-enter** with a new entry order above and size it again.
- Scale in on a continuation flag (even on the 15m) as the trade develops.
- Holding over the weekend is fine; "do not trade Mondays/Fridays" is called a myth.

## 9. Stops, risk, targets
- Stop above the top of the pattern or correction: typically **25-35 pips**; tight setups 15-25. **Minimum 15 pips** (more if you cannot watch the trade, around 20). Rarely above 50-60 (pound pairs may need more). Skip setups that need 60-80 pips; wait for a tighter entry.
- Do not tighten the stop only to improve reward-to-risk.
- **Risk is capped at 1% of the account per position** (foundation ep. 5). Never risk more because a trade "feels good".
- **Beginners: move the first trade to break even before opening a second.**
- **Exposure threshold:** maximum about **2% total open risk** at one time (some members allow up to 3%). In practice: two 1% trades open where neither has reached break even. Correlated trades (e.g. two dollar pairs) count together. Do not stack five or seven 1% trades.
- Rule-based: if the open-risk limit is used up by average trades, you cannot take the next high-probability one. Stay out of low-quality valid trades.
- Reward is uncapped; he cites a **minimum around 3:1** when thinking about the math (S3E4 used 2:1 as an average).
- Average reward-to-risk of about **3:1** is cited (an example trade showed a ~7:1 first solid target). Targets are the previous low or pattern/trendline intersection on the higher timeframe. **[OPEN]** exact target rule.

## 10. Management (S3E7): two simple processes
**A. Reduced-risk flag in the middle of a structure (not at the top/bottom):**
- After the impulse away from entry **closes solidly** (no real wick), move the stop to **break even at roughly 1-1.3%** profit. Not an exact trigger; use structure.
- Reason: the move can develop into a larger flag. Look for a **re-entry**; there are usually 1-3 chances to enter.

**B. High-probability trade at the top or bottom of a structure:**
- Be lenient. **Do not move to break even quickly, even at ~1% or more.** Price can hover/trickle, take you out at break even, then drop.
- Wait for a **significant, impulsive close/break of the structure** before locking profit or moving to break even.
- Use the **candle close**, not intrabar price, for the decision (do not move the stop on a wick).
- When price starts retracing after a significant break (retrace candles), locking a little profit and moving to break even is justified.

**Locking profit example (foundation ep. 8):** after breaking the last low of the structure, move the stop to just above that low (the line in the sand), locking about 1.8R while price was running 2.3%. Break even at about 1% after price rejected/broke the structure low.

**General:**
- Locking profit: wait for a solid impulsive close, not a hover.
- Break even is mainly a psychology and scaling tool: the worst case is a 1% loss; a missed trade is replaced by a re-entry.
- Manage consistently; expect a small error or two each month. Back-test your own management on 10 trades.
- Longer-term holds use a more relaxed approach (S3 long-term holds, not read yet).
- A 1% loss on a high-probability setup is not a mistake, just probability playing out.

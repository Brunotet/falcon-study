# Falcon strategy: rulebook, part 3 (full reads of S1E1, S1E2, S1E6, S1E7, S3E3, S3E6)

Adds to falcon.md (v6) and falcon-part2.md. Everything is what the instructor said. **[CODE]** = precise enough for the scanner. **[OPEN]** = still vague.

## 12. Nature of the market (S1E1)
- Two phases only: **impulsive** (heavy momentum, bigger moves) and **corrective** (low momentum, choppy, sideways, usually shaped like a pattern). Sequence: impulse, correction, impulse.
- Step 1 of every analysis: label the current phase. Trendlines and patterns are only a framework on top of the nature. Wrong phase labelling causes entering too early/late and FOMO.
- Zoom in as far as needed: what looks like an impulse can be a correction. A deep, messy correction is still a correction; it only delays the move. **[CODE]** phase label = share of low-momentum candles in the leg (80/20 rule, see 14).

## 13. Patterns within patterns (S1E2): the go-to filter
- Same pattern inside the same pattern raises probability: **ascending channel inside ascending channel** (sell), **descending inside descending** (buy), **bear flag inside bear flag / bull flag inside bull flag**.
- Instructor: "ascending within an ascending" at the top of a pattern/structure is his **#1 go-to setup**.
- Works on any timeframe (monthly to 15m). Check by zooming one timeframe down inside the current pattern.
- Ascending channel = reversal (down); descending channel = reversal (up). A reversal channel sitting inside a same-direction continuation adds a bonus.
- **[CODE]** score +1 when a smaller same-type channel/flag exists inside the larger one, in the trade direction.

## 14. 80/20 rule for phase (S1E2, S1E6)
- If about 80% of a move is corrective (small candles, no progression) it is a correction, even with one impulsive candle inside. Therefore the next trade is the reversal, not a continuation of that move.

## 15. Forecasting: probable vs possible (S1E6, S3E6)
- Forecast several scenarios before entering, starting on the higher timeframe, refined on the 1H. For each scenario decide the entry type in advance. Re-forecast each evening (10-15 min).
- **Probable** = the textbook completion (e.g. one-two-three, third touch). **Possible** = the earlier or variant path. The trade is taken on the possible path too if risk to reward is fine, with a management plan if it flips.
- **Do not need the third touch.** Second touch is enough for a risk entry. Overrides when the third touch is missing: (a) a **double top/bottom inside the pattern**, (b) a **tight flag of 6-8 one-hour candles** after the second-touch impulse (treat the impulse as the flag pole), (c) a **1H retrace candle** after an impulse (see falcon.md 7), (d) **evening star / doji / high-test candle** at the top as a bonus only.
- Price going slightly above the expected top is not a failed trade. It often forms a deeper version (ascending within ascending) and then drops. The instructor waits 6-8 hours of correction at the top, then places the entry order with the stop above the structure top. Backtesting taught him that price usually reaches the top first.
- Never wait for the market to satisfy a criteria list. If it is a valid structure in the right area, take it and manage it.
- **[CODE]** entry order rule on a 1H flag: after >= 6 corrective 1H candles, stop-entry just beyond the flag extreme, stop above the structure top (min 15 pips; 20 pips was used on buys, 25-35 on sells).

## 16. Watchlist and weekly process (S1E7)
- Weekly **top six** pairs (sometimes ten). Only 2-3 trades per week are needed (4-5 if active). 95-98% of his trades come from the top six.
- **Three-step process**: analysis on Friday after close (30 min), Saturday (30-60 min), Sunday (2-3 h plus the Sunday webinar). Reason: fight bias; he swaps pairs between steps and the swapped-in pair often moves 5-7%.
- Scanner implication: rank instruments by structure quality (pattern within pattern, price at outer structure top/bottom) and only look at the top few each week, not every chart. **[OPEN]** how he ranks them numerically.

## 17. Running channel and middle section (S3E6)
- **Running channel** = looks like a normal reversal channel, but the correction at its end forms a continuation pattern the other way (e.g. a descending channel ending in an ascending channel, then it drops). It signals a **continuation**, not a reversal.
- Typically in line with the higher-timeframe structure and at the top/bottom of structure (highest probability there). Less common than channels, flags, expanding formations. More common to the downside.
- Key question: **what is at the end of the pattern, and where are we within the structure?** If price came from an impulse and ends in a correction, it is a continuation.
- Use **pattern separation** and the **90% rule** target to confirm there is room. Use the 15m as a filter. Stop about 25 pips. Looks like a reversal at fake support; most recent price structure beats old support/resistance.
- **Middle section**: impulse, correction, continuation inside a one-two-three. If the middle section fails to reach the third touch, a running channel often forms and the move goes early. Still take the trade (probable vs possible), with a plan for a double top/bottom reject.
- Same pattern repeating earlier raises the probability of it repeating.
- Risk to reward must be ok (instructor example: 25 pip stop, 2.7-3% to the nearest low = about 3:1; 7:1 on a larger one).

## 18. The ten go-to setups (S3E3)
Common to all: valid structure on the daily/4H, entry on the 1H (optionally refined on the 15m), pattern within a pattern preferred, trade at the **top/bottom of a structure**.
1. **Risk entry at the top, three-touch channel with a 1H retrace candle.** Stop about 25 pips if entering after the top candle (doji), about 45-50 if waiting for the rejection candle close. 15m is used to enter after the first rejection, stop 20-25.
2. **Hover** near the top of a structure/break of an ascending channel: a tight flag on the 1H, entry after 6-8 hours, stop 25-30. Reduced risk, adds to an existing trade.
3. **Bull flag, risk entry, third touch at the bottom of a larger flag.** If the third touch does not reach the line, a **double bottom inside the pattern** is the override. Stop about 30. Target = length of the impulse. Mirror for sells.
4. **Double top within an ascending channel at the top of a structure** (risk entry, several valid entries: arc, scoop, 15m). Do not rush to break even until a significant structure break. Scale in.
5. **Double bottom within a descending channel at the bottom of a structure**, with a retrace candle. Minimum target about 7%, full about 16% in the example.
6. **Ascending within ascending at the top, risk entry.** Entry order after 6-8 corrective hours (it can take a few more hours), stop 30-40 (35 used). Target = start of the correction.
7. **Reduced-risk tight flag**: usually the **first correction after the first impulse** (second chance to get in). Stop about 25. Tight, consolidated flags only; steep ones are weaker.
8. **15m refinement of a 1H flag**: after the first impulse a correction of at least **6 fifteen-minute candles**, even a pennant. Stop 20-25 pips. Gives better reward to risk and avoids wick spike-outs.
9. **Completion of a daily structure with a one-two-three on the 15m** (scooping formation, first impulse on the 1H done). One hour has >= 7 candles; refine on 15m. Used when the setup is not picture perfect.
10. **Falcon flag**: sell/buy flag after an impulse, taken, stopped at break even, then price forms a larger version (**minimum a double top**, sometimes triple or quadruple) and then breaks. Re-enter at the top of the larger one (15m, stop about 20). Often seen with Euro-Aussie and Aussie. Hold longer for the 90% rule if relaxed.
- Targets given: beginning of the correction (90% rule) as a minimum, impulse length for flags, previous high/low of the higher-timeframe structure.
- The same set is applied to buys and sells.

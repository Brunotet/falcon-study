# Falcon strategy: rulebook, part 5 (full reads of foundation ep 3 pt 2 and ep 4)

## 23. Risk entry vs reduced-risk entry, precisely (F ep 3 pt 2)
- Risk entry = inside the pattern, nothing confirmed, you can be tagged in and out. Reduced-risk entry = on the break of a flag/pattern, market has committed. Both risk 1%.
- Trade-off, same setup: risk entry stop 60 pips for 6.5% vs reduced-risk stop above the highs for 3.3%, so the risk entry roughly **doubles reward to risk**, the reduced-risk entry costs a larger stop.
- **Do not use a reduced-risk break entry on a very large pattern** (stops of 100-200 pips). Use the smaller nested channel/flag inside it instead.
- **Tight flag (first flag) = reduced risk** on the break: entry order a couple of pips beyond the flag extreme, stop just beyond the other side (about 20-23 pips, 30 conservative), minimum **6-8 one-hour candles**, usually lasts longer.
- **Larger flag after the tight flag = risk entry** at the second bottom/top (mini double top inside the pattern), stop about 40.
- **First flag at the top of a structure (or bottom for buys) has the highest probability.** Do not try to call the top: wait for the first impulse (first commitment), then take the first flag. [CODE]: after an impulse leg of the structure break, first correction >= 6 candles, entry on break.
- Both entries often appear in one move; the instructor counts risk, reduced risk, reduced risk, reduced risk entries on one down-leg (nearly 10 entries in the CAD-Yen example). To scale in, the original trade must be at break even first.
- **Trap pattern ("catches people on the wrong side"):** price impulses up, then retraces the whole push and forms a **flag in the other direction** (e.g. bear flag after a spike up). Take that flag, not the original direction. Check 80/20: a news spike inside a corrective channel does not change the phase.
- **Failed top and trend-line break:** after breaking just above a double top (the arc) price often drops. Expanding structure with a double top: still valid.
- **Retrace candle entry (risk entry):** about 25 pip stop, target at least the 90% rule (the start of the correction); near 9:1 in the Euro-Pound example. Never stop below 15 pips.
- 15m is not for beginners (see part 4).

## 24. Stops and targets (F ep 3 pt 2)
- Stop goes just above the structure/flag top (23-30 pips typical). Do not put the entry or stop extremely close to price "to get a tight stop".
- Do not halve the stop to reach a profit number. Do not set imaginary targets. No fixed 1:1. Minimum goal 3:1, use structure to squeeze (first solid target examples: previous low, trendline, 90% rule fill).
- Profit targets only when you cannot watch (flight, overnight, short-term trade into a double top/bottom).

## 25. Probability, edge and evaluation (F ep 4)
- Edge = weighted coin. Win/loss order is unknown; only the large sample matters. Example month at 3:1 with 3 wins, 5 losses, 2 break evens is still +4%.
- **Evaluate quarterly (or over about 30 trades), not daily/weekly/monthly.** Backtests with a few dozen trades are noise.
- Break evens are a normal third outcome. Count wins, losses and break evens separately in a backtest.
- Keep **80% high-probability, 20% valid** trades. Too many low-quality valid trades (about 1:1 each) turn a good month into about +1%.
- **Do not trade in a corrective period just to trade.** Step back when the market is forming deeper corrections/new patterns. Trade when the structure gives permission.
- Over-trading, revenge trades, banking at 1% instead of 3:1, forcing trades near a monthly target are the named mistakes.
- A loss on a trade that met the plan is not a mistake; a win on a bad trade is not a success.
- Average 10 trades per month is the illustrative number; 12-15 also fine.
